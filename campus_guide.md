# Campus Guide

All names, rules, prices, rooms and codes in this guide are invented for a classroom lab.

## About this guide
This guide answers common questions from students. It covers registration, fees, exams, IT services, the library, transport, housing and student life. Offices are open Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 16:00, unless a section says otherwise. If your question is not answered here, contact Student Services in Building A, room A-101, or write to help@campus.example.

## Course registration
Students choose their courses on the student portal during the registration window. The window opens in week 13 of the previous semester and closes at midnight on the Friday of week 1. Your academic advisor must approve your course list before the portal lets you confirm it. If a course is full, add yourself to the waiting list; seats are offered in waiting-list order until the end of week 1.

## Late registration
Students who register after the registration window closes must pay a late registration fee of AED 300. Late registration is only possible until the Thursday of week 2, and only with written approval from your advisor and the head of department. After week 2 you cannot register for the semester, and you must apply for a leave of absence instead.

## Adding and dropping courses
During the add and drop period, which ends on the Thursday of week 2, you can add or drop courses on the portal without any note on your transcript. A course dropped in this period disappears from your record. Check the refund rules before you drop a course, because the amount you get back depends on the week.

## Withdrawing from a course
After the add and drop period, you can still withdraw from a course until the end of week 10. A withdrawn course stays on your transcript with the grade W, which does not affect your GPA. Withdrawal after week 10 is only allowed for documented medical or family emergencies, with approval from the Dean of Students. Withdrawn courses are not refunded.

## Refunds
If you drop a course, part of the tuition is returned to you. Courses dropped in week 1 receive a 100% refund. Courses dropped in week 2 receive a 50% refund. From week 3 onwards there is no refund. Refunds are paid to the original payment method within 14 working days. Fees for late registration, parking permits and ID cards are never refunded.

## Tuition fees
Undergraduate tuition is AED 1,150 per credit hour. A typical full-time load of 15 credit hours costs AED 17,250 per semester. Tuition is paid in two instalments: the first by the end of week 1 and the second by the end of week 8. Students who miss an instalment deadline pay a late payment fee of AED 200, and their portal access is blocked until the payment is made.

## How to pay
Fees can be paid by bank transfer, by card at the Finance Office in Building A, or online through the ePay page of the student portal. Cash is not accepted anywhere on campus. When you pay by bank transfer, write your student ID in the payment reference, otherwise the payment cannot be matched to your account. Receipts appear on the portal within two working days.

## Scholarships
Merit scholarships cover 25%, 50% or 100% of tuition. To keep a merit scholarship, you must earn a semester GPA of at least 3.30 and complete at least 15 credit hours every semester. If your GPA falls below 3.30, the scholarship is suspended for one semester, not cancelled; it returns when your GPA recovers. Applications for new scholarships open in week 4 and close in week 6.

## Financial hardship support
Students facing a sudden financial difficulty, such as the loss of a family income, can apply to the Student Emergency Fund. The fund can cover up to AED 6,000 of tuition per academic year and does not need to be paid back. Apply with Form EF-9 and supporting documents at Student Services. Applications are confidential and are decided within ten working days.

## Sibling discount
If two or more brothers or sisters study at the university at the same time, the second and every further sibling receive a 10% discount on tuition. The discount is not automatic: apply once with Form SD-2 and a copy of the family book. The discount cannot be combined with a 100% merit scholarship.

## Attendance
Attendance is recorded in every class. If you miss more than 15% of the classes in a course, you receive a written warning. If you miss more than 25%, you receive the grade FA (failure due to absence) and cannot sit the final exam. Absences with an approved medical excuse do not count towards these limits.

## Medical excuses
If illness stops you from attending a class, test or exam, submit a medical certificate from a licensed clinic to Student Services within three working days, using Form ME-1. Certificates submitted later are not accepted. For a missed final exam with an approved excuse, you sit a make-up exam in the first week of the next semester.

## Final exams
Final exams take place in weeks 15 and 16. The exam timetable is published on the portal in week 10. Arrive 15 minutes before the start and bring your student ID card; without it you cannot enter the exam room. Phones and smart watches must be switched off and left in your bag at the front of the room. You may not leave during the first 30 minutes.

## Make-up exams
A make-up exam is offered only to students with an approved excuse. Make-up exams for midterms are held in week 9, and make-up exams for finals in the first week of the next semester. There is no fee for a make-up exam. A student who misses a make-up exam receives a mark of zero.

## Re-sit exam for graduating students
If you fail one course in your final semester and it is the only course you still need to graduate, you may take a re-sit exam within four weeks after grades are published. The re-sit fee is AED 250, and the highest grade you can receive is C.

## Exam clashes
If two of your final exams are scheduled at the same time, report the clash to the Examinations Office within five working days after the timetable is published. The office moves one of the exams to a clash session. Clashes reported later are not moved.

## Grades and results
Grades are published on the portal seven working days after the end of the final exam period. The grading scale runs from A (4.0) to F (0.0). A grade of D (1.0) is a pass, but some programmes require at least C (2.0) in core courses.

## Grade appeals
If you believe a final grade is wrong, you can appeal within five working days after grades are published. Submit Form GA-3 to the Examinations Office and pay a fee of AED 100. A second examiner reviews your paper. If your grade changes, the fee is returned. The decision of the appeals committee is final.

## Academic integrity
Cheating and plagiarism are serious offences. Copying from another student, using unauthorised notes or devices in an exam, or submitting work written by someone else, including an AI tool when the course does not allow it, are all violations. A first violation gives zero marks for the assessment. A second violation gives the grade XF for the course, and a third leads to dismissal from the university.

## Academic probation
If your cumulative GPA falls below 2.00, you are placed on academic probation. On probation you may take at most 12 credit hours and must meet your advisor every two weeks. If your GPA is still below 2.00 after two semesters on probation, you are dismissed from your programme.

## Repeating a course
You can repeat a course you failed or passed with a D. Only the higher grade counts in your GPA, but both attempts stay on your transcript. A course can be taken at most three times.

## Changing your major
To move to another programme, submit Form PT-5 to the Registrar after your first semester. You need a cumulative GPA of at least 2.50, and the new department must have places. Courses you have passed are transferred if they belong to the new study plan.

## Semester withdrawal
If you want to stop studying in the middle of a semester, you can withdraw from all your courses at once with Form SW-6 until the end of week 10. All courses then get the grade W. Tuition is returned only under the normal refund rules.

## Leave of absence
If you need a break from your studies, you can take a leave of absence of one or two semesters. Submit Form LA-2 to the Registrar before the end of week 2 of the semester you want to miss. During a leave you keep your student ID number and your place in your programme, but you cannot use campus services. A leave of more than two semesters requires readmission.

## English placement test
New students whose English score is below the admission level take the English placement test in the week before classes. Depending on the result, you start with ENG 099 (Foundation English) or go directly to ENG 101. ENG 099 does not count towards your degree.

## Credit load
A full-time student takes between 12 and 18 credit hours per semester. Students with a cumulative GPA of 3.50 or higher may take up to 21 credit hours with their advisor's approval. In the summer term the maximum is 9 credit hours.

## Graduation requirements
To graduate with a bachelor's degree you must complete 132 credit hours, including an internship, with a cumulative GPA of at least 2.00. Apply for graduation on the portal in week 4 of your final semester. The graduation ceremony is held once a year, in June.

## Internship
All bachelor's students complete an internship of at least 8 weeks (240 hours) after passing 90 credit hours. The Careers Centre keeps a list of approved employers, or you can propose your own. Your employer supervisor fills in an evaluation form at the end, and you submit a written report.

## Transcripts and certificates
An official transcript costs AED 50 and is ready in three working days. Request it on the portal with Form TR-1. Letters confirming that you are a student, for example for a bank or an embassy, are free and are issued on the same day by Student Services.

## Student ID card
Your student ID card is your key to the library, the computer labs, the sports centre and the exam rooms. If you lose your card, report it on the portal at once so it can be blocked. A replacement card costs AED 75 and is ready the next working day at Student Services.

## Student email and accounts
Every student gets a university account. Your username is your student ID number, and your email address is your student ID followed by @students.campus.example. The account gives you email, the portal, the learning platform and Wi-Fi. Passwords expire every 120 days. To reset a forgotten password, use the self-service page at reset.campus.example; you will need the phone number you registered.

## Wi-Fi
The student network is CampusNet. Log in with your university username and password. Guests can use CampusGuest, which needs a one-day code from the reception desk. If CampusNet does not connect, forget the network on your device and join again; if it still fails, report it to the IT Service Desk.

## Wi-Fi error codes
If CampusNet shows error W-17, your password has expired: change it at reset.campus.example and connect again. Error W-22 means your device is not on the list of allowed devices: add it on the IT page of the portal, up to three devices per student. Error W-31 means the network is full in this area: move to another floor or try again in a few minutes.

## Portal error codes
Error PX-401 means your session has expired: log in again. Error PX-403 means you tried to open a page that is not available to you, usually a course you are not registered in: check your registration. Error PX-429 means too many attempts in a short time: wait 15 minutes before you try again. Error PX-500 means a fault on our side: try again later and report it if it lasts more than one hour.

## Learning platform
Course materials, assignments and online quizzes are on the learning platform, LearnHub. Log in with your university account. Assignments are submitted as PDF files of at most 20 MB. If an upload fails with error LH-08, the file is too large. Error LH-15 means the deadline has passed, and only your teacher can reopen the submission.

## Software for students
Students can install Microsoft 365 on up to five devices for free. MATLAB, SPSS and the Adobe Creative Cloud apps are installed in the computer labs; MATLAB can also be installed on your own laptop with a campus licence. Download links are on the Software page of the portal. Licences end when you graduate.

## Computer labs
The computer labs are in Building C, rooms C-101 to C-112. They are open from 8:00 to 22:00, Monday to Friday, and from 14:00 to 20:00 on Saturday. Room C-110 stays open 24 hours during the final exam period. Food and drinks are not allowed in the labs. Log in with your university account.

## Printing
Printers are in the library and in every computer lab. Black-and-white printing costs AED 0.20 per page and colour costs AED 1 per page. Top up your print credit at the library desk or on the portal. If a printer shows error PR-07, the paper tray is empty. Error PR-12 means a paper jam: ask the lab assistant and do not open the printer yourself.

## IT Service Desk
The IT Service Desk is in Building C, room C-001. It is open from 8:00 to 18:00, Monday to Friday. You can also report a problem by email to itdesk@campus.example or by phone on extension 4357. If your laptop is stolen, also report the theft to Campus Security.

## Library
The library is in Building B. It is open from 8:00 to 23:00, Monday to Friday, and from 10:00 to 18:00 on Saturday. It is closed on Sundays. Undergraduate students may borrow up to 5 books at a time for 21 days, and can renew a loan twice online if nobody has reserved the book.

## Library fines
Books returned late are charged AED 2 per book per day, up to a maximum of AED 60 per book. A lost or damaged book is charged at its replacement price plus a processing fee of AED 25. While you have unpaid library fines, you cannot borrow books or receive your transcript.

## Laptop loans
The library lends laptops for use inside the library. Borrow one at the service desk with your student ID card for up to four hours; the laptops cannot leave the building. A late return is charged AED 10 per hour.

## Lockers
Lockers are available on the first floor of the library for one semester at a time. Book a locker at the library desk for AED 30 per semester, and empty it before the last day of final exams; items left after that are removed.

## Group study rooms
The library has twelve group study rooms on the second floor, each with a screen and a whiteboard. Groups of three to eight students can book a room for up to three hours per day on the library website. If your group does not arrive within 15 minutes, the booking is cancelled.

## Student housing
The university has two halls of residence on campus: Hall A for female students and Hall D for male students. A shared room costs AED 9,000 per semester and a single room AED 13,500, including water, electricity and internet. Apply on the Housing page of the portal before 1 July for the autumn semester. Places are offered first to students whose family lives more than 100 km away.

## Hall rules
In the halls of residence, visitors may stay in the common rooms until 21:00. Overnight guests are not allowed. Quiet hours are from 23:00 to 7:00. Cooking is allowed only in the shared kitchens, not in bedrooms. Students who break hall rules can lose their housing place.

## Shuttle bus B1
Route B1 runs between the campus main gate and the City Centre bus station. Buses leave every 20 minutes from 7:00 to 21:00, Monday to Friday. The ride is free with your student ID card.

## Shuttle bus B2
Route B2 runs on Saturdays and Sundays between the halls of residence and the Corniche shopping area. Buses leave every hour from 10:00 to 23:00, and the last bus back to campus leaves the Corniche at 23:30. The ride is free with your student ID card.

## Night shuttle N1
During the final exam period, a night shuttle, route N1, runs from the library to the halls of residence every 30 minutes between 22:00 and 2:00. The ride is free with your student ID card.

## Counselling and wellbeing
Free and confidential counselling is available to all students. Counsellors can help with stress, anxiety, homesickness, sleep problems and personal difficulties. Book an appointment by email at wellbeing@campus.example or walk in to Building F, room F-012, between 9:00 and 15:00. In an emergency outside these hours, call Campus Security on extension 999.

## Campus clinic
The campus clinic is in Building F, ground floor. A nurse is available from 8:00 to 17:00, Monday to Friday, and a doctor visits on Monday and Wednesday mornings. Basic medicines are free. The clinic can issue a medical certificate only for an illness it has treated itself.

## Disability and learning support
Students with a disability, a long-term health condition or a learning difficulty such as dyslexia can receive support, for example extra time in exams, a separate exam room or help with note-taking. Register with the Accessibility Office in Building A, room A-115, at least four weeks before the exam period, with a medical report.

## Sports centre
The sports centre in Building G has a gym, two indoor courts and a running track. It is open from 7:00 to 22:00 every day, with women-only hours from 9:00 to 13:00. Entry is free with your student ID card. Lockers are available, but bring your own lock.

## Clubs and societies
There are more than 40 student clubs, from robotics and debating to photography and cultural societies. To start a new club, you need at least 15 members and a faculty advisor; submit the proposal to the Student Activities Office in week 3. Clubs can apply for a budget of up to AED 2,000 per semester.

## Parking
Students may park only in zone P3, behind the sports centre, with a valid parking permit. A permit costs AED 400 per academic year and is ordered on the portal. Cars parked without a permit or outside zone P3 receive a fine of AED 150.

## Prayer rooms
Prayer rooms for men and women are on the ground floor of every building. Ablution areas are next to each prayer room. During Ramadan, the university announces shorter class times on the portal.

## Food on campus
The Food Court in Building B is open from 7:30 to 19:00, Monday to Friday. There are cafés in Buildings A and C. Students pay by card or with credit on their student ID card. Microwaves for students are on the first floor of Building B.

## Visitors and guests
Visitors must register at the main gate and wear a visitor badge. A student may bring at most two guests at a time. Guests may not enter computer labs or exam rooms. Guests can use the CampusGuest Wi-Fi network.

## Lost and found
Items found on campus are taken to the Security Office at the main gate and kept for 30 days. To collect an item, bring your student ID card and describe the item. Items that nobody collects are donated after 30 days.

## Campus security and emergencies
Campus Security works 24 hours a day from the Security Office at the main gate. In an emergency, call extension 999 from any campus phone or 07 555 0199 from a mobile. Fire assembly points are marked with green signs outside every building.

## Volunteering
Students can earn a Volunteering Certificate by completing 40 hours of approved community service. Record your hours on the Volunteering page of the portal; the Student Activities Office approves them. The certificate is listed in your graduation documents.

## Careers Centre
The Careers Centre helps with CVs, interview practice and finding internships and jobs. The yearly career fair takes place in the second week of March, with more than 80 employers. Book a CV review on the portal; appointments last 30 minutes.

## International students
The International Office in Building A helps international students with their student residence visa. Start the renewal at least 30 days before your visa expires, with a valid passport, a recent photo and proof of registration. A late renewal can lead to fines from the immigration authority, which the student must pay.

## Student complaints
If you have a complaint about a service or a staff member, first try to solve it with the office concerned. If that does not work, submit a formal complaint on the portal with Form SC-8. You will receive a reply within ten working days. Complaints are confidential, and no student is treated unfairly because of a complaint.

## Summer term
The summer term lasts six weeks and starts in June. You may take at most 9 credit hours. Summer registration opens in week 12 of the spring semester. Summer courses cost the same per credit hour as courses in the regular semesters.

## Student council
The student council has 12 members, elected every October by all students. Any student with a cumulative GPA of at least 2.50 can stand for election. The council meets the university management twice a semester.

## Course evaluations
At the end of every semester you can evaluate each of your courses anonymously on the portal in weeks 13 and 14. Teachers see only a summary, and only after grades are published.

## Academic calendar
The autumn semester runs from late August to December and the spring semester from January to May. Each semester has 16 weeks: 14 weeks of teaching followed by two weeks of final exams. A short summer term of six weeks starts in June. Public holidays are announced on the portal.

## Academic advising
Every student has an academic advisor from their department. Meet your advisor at least once each semester, before registration. Your advisor approves your course list, helps you plan your studies and can refer you to other services.
